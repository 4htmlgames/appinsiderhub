<h2>Why an Earthquake in NYC Is More Likely Than You Think</h2>

<p>New York City sits near the Ramapo Fault System, a centuries-old seismic zone stretching from Pennsylvania through New Jersey into New York. USGS records show that the tri-state area experiences several measurable earthquakes every year — most too small to feel, but some capable of causing real structural damage to the city's aging building stock. The 2024 event was a sharp reminder: <strong>earthquake in NYC</strong> is not a hypothetical. It's a recurring reality that demands preparation.</p>

<p>The problem isn't just the quake itself. It's the cascade: aftershocks, potential infrastructure failures, and the flood of unverified information that overwhelms social media within minutes. World Monitor cuts through all of that by pulling directly from <strong>USGS</strong> and verified global monitoring networks, giving you source-confirmed data — magnitude, depth, epicenter, and aftershock probability — before mainstream media even assigns a reporter.</p>
